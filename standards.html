Community Standards

Who handles what

HOA & homeowner responsibilities

Association responsibilities generally include

  • Common Area maintenance, repair and replacement.
  • Association-owned fencing, bulkheads and common improvements.
  • Community-wide administration, rules and enforcement.
  • Yard maintenance only to the scope and frequency established by the Board.
  • Community projects specifically authorized by the Board.

Homeowner responsibilities generally include

  • Exterior cleanliness, pressure washing or equivalent cleaning.
  • Gutters, shutters, windows, doors and private driveways.
  • Landscaping not covered by Association yard service.
  • Trees and vegetation located on the owner's lot unless otherwise designated.
  • Contractor cleanup, private debris removal and tenant/guest compliance.

Shared townhome components

Owners maintain private HVAC equipment and supports, roofs and other private improvements. Owners sharing party walls and roof boundary lines must coordinate maintenance, access and cost contributions under Declaration §§8.25–8.28. Report uncertainty about shared components to management before arranging work.

Landscaping and infrastructure

The Association arranges routine yard service within the Board-defined scope, including common grounds and contracted leaf removal. Owners maintain private areas outside that scope. Report erosion, exposed roots, drainage problems, damaged perimeter fencing, bulkheads or common irrigation/backflow equipment to management. Do not alter shared infrastructure.

Declaration §§3.01, 6.04, 8.19, 8.25–8.28.

2
Curb appeal matters

Exterior cleanliness & property appearance

Every homeowner contributes to the overall appearance and value of the community. Homes should remain reasonably clean and well maintained.

Exterior cleaning

Visible mildew, algae, heavy dirt, staining, discoloration or similar neglect should be corrected. Pressure washing is one option, but any safe and effective cleaning method is acceptable. The standard is the result: a clean, properly maintained exterior.

Front-yard appearance

Front yards have an outsized effect on the community's appearance. Prolonged visible clutter, neglected exterior items and other unsightly conditions may be addressed by the Association.

Tree stumps

When a homeowner arranges tree removal, roadway-visible stumps should be removed or ground. Report stumps left by Association contractors to management. Follow any separately communicated compliance deadline.

Windows, screens and blinds

Maintain screens, shutters, windows and visible blinds in good repair. Existing architectural guidelines call for crooked or broken blinds to be remedied or removed within seven days of notice. Keep front yards free of persistent clutter.

Community goal: clean and attractive, not perfection. The Board has historically focused on material issues rather than trivial or cosmetic nitpicking.

Declaration §§8.07, 8.25–8.27; Architectural Review Guidelines. Stump removal is a Board appearance standard, not an express deadline in the Declaration.

What belongs in the dumpster

The dumpster is for ordinary household garbage contained in fully closed, normal-size trash bags.

What does not belong in or around the dumpster

  • Furniture, mattresses or appliances.
  • Doors, cabinets or renovation debris.
  • Contractor-generated waste.
  • Yard debris or tree material.
  • Loose trash, loose glass or food waste.
  • Hazardous materials.
  • Cardboard.
  • Anything left on the ground beside or around the dumpster enclosure.
If the dumpster is full: we recommend that you please wait for the next scheduled pickup. Do not stack material above capacity and do not leave bags, bulk items or debris outside the enclosure.

Overflow, unauthorized bulk disposal and special cleanup cost the entire Association money. Repeated unnecessary costs can contribute to higher assessments over time.

If you safely observe prohibited dumping or material being left outside the enclosure, report it to management or the Board. Photos are helpful when they can be obtained without confrontation.

The cardboard prohibition and bag-only rule are Board/vendor policy. Recycling notices are typically posted at the broader Wando East entrance behind CVS near Mathis Ferry Road. Declaration §8.12 and Architectural Review Guidelines address dumpster use.

4
Plan guest parking

Parking, guests & vehicle storage

Routine street parking is prohibited. There is no designated overflow parking area, so vehicles should be parked on the owner's lot or driveway.

  • No regular on-street parking.
  • No parking on grass.
  • No parking over rolled curbs or in a manner that creates an obstruction or nuisance.
  • Vehicle-storage restrictions in the governing documents also apply to certain trailers, campers, boats and commercial vehicles.

Short-term guests

The Association has traditionally exercised reasonable discretion for an occasional overnight guest or someone visiting for a few days. That courtesy does not create permission for recurring or long-term street parking.

If a vehicle remains on the street repeatedly or for an extended period, particularly after a complaint, management or the Board may monitor the condition, contact the homeowner and enforce the restriction if it continues.

Practical tip: coordinate guest parking in advance. With permission, a neighbor may be willing to provide an available driveway space for a short visit.

Declaration §8.18 prohibits street parking except on a single-event basis. Discretion for short visits is not a standing exemption. Architectural Review Guidelines also address grass and rolled-curb parking.

5
Protect shared property

Trees, vegetation, fences & bulkheads

Association-owned fencing, bulkheads and similar common improvements are community property. Unless written authorization has been provided, homeowners should not attach items to them, lean materials against them, modify them or obstruct access to them.

Trees and vegetation located on a homeowner's lot remain that homeowner's responsibility between any community-wide pruning projects. Branches, vines and shrubs should be maintained so they do not press against or damage Association fencing or bulkheads, block drainage, or interfere with access.

Periodic community work: the Association may arrange pruning or other shared maintenance from time to time. That does not permanently transfer routine responsibility for privately located trees or vegetation to the HOA.
6
Access must remain open

Fencing, easements & rear access

Fencing is tightly regulated. Do not construct, relocate, expand, replace or materially alter fencing without the required written ARC / ARB approval.

The 2013 fencing resolution specifically addresses designated even-numbered Lauda Drive units and establishes permitted fencing type, height and placement. Existing fencing elsewhere in the community does not automatically establish permission for another property.

10-foot access standard

Board policy requires maintaining approximately 10 feet of access along the applicable rear/common access areas, including approximately 10 feet beyond community fencing on one side of the community and approximately 10 feet beyond the bulkhead on the other side. This access exists so City personnel, emergency responders, contractors and authorized Association representatives can reach infrastructure and common property when needed.

  • Do not block the access area with permanent structures.
  • Do not allow trees or vegetation to make the access impassable.
  • Do not store materials where they interfere with access.
  • Small, easily removable barriers previously approved by the ARC / ARB remain subject to the terms of their individual approval.

The 10-foot clearance is the Board’s operating access standard, not a blanket recorded easement dimension. Actual legal easements depend on recorded plats and Declaration §§12.01–12.02. Non-emergency access under §12.02 is during daylight hours with reasonable advance notice; emergency repair access may be immediate. The 2013 resolution separately specifies four-foot black aluminum fencing for designated units and placement ten feet from the applicable deck end. Consult management before any fence work.

7
Ask before changing the exterior

Architectural review (ARC / ARB)

Exterior additions or changes generally require review and written approval before work begins. This includes changes to fencing, doors, visible window treatments, exterior colors or materials, landscaping that changes appearance, parking areas and other visible improvements.

Current guideline reminders

  • Planters: terracotta, ceramic or concrete; plastic planters are not allowed. Faux terracotta/concrete may be considered case-by-case.
  • Storm doors: approved full-glass style with white trim.
  • Front benches require approval.
  • Garden hoses and hose apparatus should not remain visible for more than 24 hours.
  • Wind chimes are not allowed in front.
  • Recycle containers should not remain visible except for recycling day.
  • Coolers should not remain visible in front for more than 24 hours.
  • Carports are not allowed.
  • For-sale and for-rent signs should be restricted to window placement.
  • Crooked or broken blinds visible from outside should be repaired or removed after notice.
Submitting an architectural request: Submit ARC / ARB requests through the homeowner portal. Obtain written approval before beginning work.

Other exterior projects

Seek written approval before screened porches, decks, exterior material or color changes and other visible alterations. The Declaration restricts sheds, outbuildings and certain vehicle storage; do not assume an existing installation establishes permission. Municipal permits may also be required.

Declaration §§8.14, 8.21, 8.24–8.27 and Article XI.

8
Reasonable first, enforcement when needed

Enforcement philosophy

The Association's goal is not to nitpick or penalize homeowners unnecessarily. In the history of the community, the Board's general approach has been to focus on meaningful issues that affect appearance, safety, access, shared expenses or fair compliance.

  1. A concern is identified or reported.
  2. The responsible homeowner is contacted when appropriate.
  3. A reasonable correction period is provided based on the specific situation.
  4. The issue is reviewed again.
  5. Fines or other permitted enforcement may be assessed when a violation remains unresolved or circumstances warrant it.

Compliance timeframes are determined case-by-case because replacing a damaged item, cleaning a home, correcting landscaping and removing an immediate safety hazard are not equivalent tasks.

Homeowners are responsible for ensuring that tenants, household members, guests and contractors comply with community requirements.

Pets and neighborly conduct

Dogs must be leashed whenever outside a fenced yard. Promptly collect and sanitarily dispose of pet waste. Avoid nuisance conditions and do not burn trash, leaves or rubbish. Owners are responsible for tenant compliance. Report concerns privately rather than confronting neighbors.

Noise and quiet enjoyment

Residents and guests should keep music, outdoor gatherings, vehicles and equipment at reasonable sound levels, particularly in the evening. The Town of Mount Pleasant Code of Ordinances, §132.03, prohibits excessive noise at any hour and generally establishes the following sound limits, subject to its measurement rules and exceptions:

  • Sunday through Thursday: 60 dB from 8 a.m. to 9 p.m.; 55 dB overnight.
  • Friday, Saturday and federal or state holidays: 60 dB from 8 a.m. to 10 p.m.; 55 dB overnight (holiday schedule takes precedence).

Building operations are prohibited between 9 p.m. and 7 a.m., subject to applicable exceptions. Lawn equipment has a conditional exception from 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. Music or amplified sound plainly audible inside another residence may violate the ordinance even during daytime hours. These are municipal requirements, not new HOA quiet hours.

For an active non-emergency noise complaint, contact police dispatch at 843-743-7200. For emergencies, call 911. Recurring community nuisance concerns may also be reported privately through management.

Source: Town of Mount Pleasant Code of Ordinances §132.03. Consult the current ordinance for full definitions, exceptions and enforcement provisions.

Recreational fires and fire safety

Small recreational fires using clean, dry, untreated wood, including sticks and branches suitable as firewood, are acceptable only where permitted by applicable fire regulations and Association requirements. Never burn yard waste, treated wood or rubbish. Use an appropriate fire pit, maintain safe clearance from buildings, fences and vegetation, and avoid windy or dry conditions and burn bans. Keep water or an extinguisher nearby, supervise the fire continuously and extinguish it completely before leaving. Avoid smoke or other nuisance conditions affecting neighbors.

Declaration §§8.08, 8.23. Recreational fire guidance is subject to applicable fire regulations.

9
Participation is welcome

Meetings, ideas & staying informed

The Board typically schedules the annual membership meeting near year-end, often in December. The governing Bylaws permit the annual meeting during November or at another time established by the Board. Always rely on the official meeting notice for the actual date and location.

Homeowners are strongly encouraged to attend, ask questions, discuss projects and finances, and suggest improvements. Practical ideas for landscaping, lighting, safety, beautification, waste management and community operations are welcome throughout the year.

Keep your email address, phone number, mailing address, ownership information and tenant information current with the management company. Review Association emails, the current management portal and this website regularly.

Meeting notices, project schedules and compliance deadlines are distributed through official management communications, including mailed annual meeting notices as required. This website does not publish a live calendar.

Governing document changes

Proposed modernization of restrictions, rental provisions or architectural standards is not an adopted change. Declaration §13.03 generally requires approval by owners holding at least 51% of lots for amendments, subject to applicable requirements. Consult management for the current recorded documents and amendments.

Important: This site is a plain-English community reference. It does not replace or amend the recorded Declaration, Bylaws, plats, easements, Board resolutions, architectural guidelines or applicable law. If a conflict exists, the controlling document or law prevails.
